# Twin Queens

*Chess on a 9×8 board with two queens on each side. New chess with empty ratings and no theory: everyone starts from zero.*

## Board and setup

The board has 9 files (a–i) and 8 ranks. Each side has 9 pawns and the following pieces on the back rank, from a to i:

**Rook, Knight, Bishop, Queen, King, Queen, Knight, Bishop, Rook**

The two bishops stand on squares of different colors, so each side keeps one light-squared and one dark-squared bishop.

## Rules

- All pieces move exactly as in standard chess.
- Pawns move one square forward, or two from their starting rank, capture diagonally, and can capture en passant.
- A pawn that reaches the last rank promotes to a queen, rook, bishop or knight.
- Checkmate wins. Stalemate, threefold repetition, the 50-move rule and insufficient material are draws.

## Castling

- **Kingside:** the king moves from e to g, and the rook from i jumps to f.
- **Queenside:** the king moves from e to c, and the rook from a jumps to d.

The usual conditions apply: the king and the rook have not moved, the squares between them are empty, and the king is not in check and does not pass through or land on an attacked square.

## Why it is different

Two queens per side make the opening sharp and tactics come early. Opening theory does not exist yet, so every game is a chance to discover something new.
